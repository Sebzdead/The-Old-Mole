## TL;DR

- **We re-released the World School as edited cuts and almost nobody came.** 22 re-cut talks dropped simultaneously on 08-14 pulled **2,086 views between them** — an average of 95 each, against 736–4,926 for the raw versions a week earlier. The edit fixed last week's loudest complaint and the fix reached ~2% of the audience that complained.
- **The one talk released on its own recovered nearly all its original demand.** "Multi-Polarity and Marxism", re-cut and posted alone on 08-12, did **1,619 views** — 95% of the raw version's 1,696 — and accounted for **44% of all upload views and 56% of all upload watch-time** this week. It also had ~2.3× the in-window exposure of the 08-14 batch, so treat the multiple as directional, not exact.
- **Channel down across the board off the World School peak:** views **27,269 (−67.5%)**, watch time **419,601 min (−70.7%)**, net subs **+153 (−75.3%)**. Avg view duration held up far better at **15:54 (−8.0%)** — the audience that showed up still watched long.
- **The edit was the right call and viewers said so unprompted:** "i really appreciate these cut versions! Will be watching the ones i missed" (3 likes), "I really appreciate the editor cutting out pauses, makes the world school videos a lot more watchable!!" One substantive critique: sentences are being cut mid-flow and transitions need smoothing.
- **Our comment sections are now a moderation problem, not just a discussion.** On "Is The World Going Fascist?" roughly half of 17 comments are hostile, and one account posted antisemitic conspiracy material under **five separate uploads** this week. This is the second week running that conspiracy is filling the explanatory vacuum — now on our own property.
- **Instagram is carrying the week.** The "Workers of the world, unite!" reel hit **44,205 reach / 71,733 views / 6,275 likes / 1,021 shares**; the Cuba reel **28,538 reach / 47,822 views / 3,105 likes / 786 shares**. Between them, ~10× our YouTube upload views.
- **The sharpest thing anyone said to us this week was a challenge, not praise:** under the Cuba reel, "Any actual orgs that you sponsor so people can donate supplies to Cuba… Or do you just like shouting 'down with imperialism' and doing nothing else?" (8 likes).
- **Climate arrived in the landscape as a price, not a forecast.** Novara's El Niño explainer drew 1,042 likes on a comment about Thames Water losing half a billion litres a day amid a third drought year; drought, crop failure and food shortages ran through a dozen of their videos.
- **Data centres are the emerging enclosure fight**, and the audience is already politicising it: Novara's water piece drew "So how long until any anti-data-centre movements are designated as terrorist organisations…?" (679 likes) as its top comment.
- **Second Thought's China video produced the week's biggest single audience signal outside our own channels** — 2,854 likes on "we're getting the mass surveillance, censorship, and one-party government without the treats". The campist question is live and being answered badly.

## The Landscape

Novara Media supplied 33 of 53 videos this week, so the weighting below skews British and skews toward Novara's framing. Where a theme rests on a single channel, that is stated.

### Climate as a grocery bill

Drought, heatwave and wildfire stopped being weather coverage and became price coverage: crop failures, forecast food shortages, "the UK heatwave has already cost billions". The contradiction is that capital must externalise climate cost to remain profitable, so the cost returns to the working class in the only form it can — the wage's declining purchasing power. What is new is that the mediation is now visible to the audience in real time: "'we need to drill for oil to bring down the cost of living' — well this drought is the effect of that logic" (445 likes).
**Signal: HIGH**

### Water as private rent

Thames Water losing half a billion litres a day while households are rationed drew the week's largest single comment score in the landscape (1,042 likes). A privatised natural monopoly cannot invest in infrastructure whose returns fall outside the dividend horizon; scarcity is not a failure of the model but its operating condition. "Privatisation is the root cause of this current situation. Everything is about maximising share holder value."
**Signal: HIGH**

### Data centres as new enclosure

AI infrastructure is exempted from the water and energy restrictions imposed on the population — the state rations citizens and not capital. Two Novara videos, and the audience politicised it faster than the coverage did: the top comment is a prediction that anti-data-centre organising will be designated terrorism (679 likes), the second-tier comments connect it to eminent domain seizures in the US and to surveillance. This is a live organising terrain with no left-wing analysis occupying it yet.
**Signal: HIGH**

### Infrastructural decay as social contract

Vape shops, betting shops, bursting prisons, a student loan book that cannot be repaid, shrinkflation. The unifying dynamic is rentier extraction from socialised infrastructure with no counterpart investment: value is pulled out of the built environment and the public purse without being returned. The most-liked comment in the whole core corpus for this theme is one sentence — "The social contract is broken from top to bottom" (160 likes).
**Signal: HIGH**

### Reformism absorbing insurgency

**Signal: HIGH**

### The right as beneficiary of failure

## Podcast Angles

### 1. Bonapartism, not fascism: what the state is actually becoming

Take the definitional fight happening in our own comment section and settle it. A viewer already supplied the frame — Trotsky's Bonapartism, "a military-police dictatorship barely concealed with the dressings of bourgeois democracy" — and it explains Channel 5's free-speech slate, the prison overcrowding, and the arrests for holding placards far better than "are we going fascist?" does.
**Why now:** our two highest-liked comments this week are both viewers reaching for a more precise category than the one our own title offered them.

### 2. Who pays for the drought?

Third drought year, half a billion litres a day lost by a privatised monopoly, crop failure feeding straight into food prices. The Marxist question is not "is this the climate crisis" but "why is a natural monopoly whose returns are dividends structurally incapable of building the infrastructure that would prevent this" — and what a planned water system would actually look like.
**Why now:** Novara's audience produced a 1,042-like comment doing the first half of this analysis and stopping exactly where we would begin.

### 3. Data centres and the new enclosures

Capital claims water and power by right while households are rationed by decree. Frame it as enclosure — the privatisation of a commons under conditions of scarcity, backed by state force — and take seriously the audience's own prediction that resistance to it will be criminalised.
**Why now:** 679 likes on "how long until any anti-data-centre movements are designated as terrorist organisations" means the political question is already being asked and no one on the left has answered it.

### 4. "Multiple masters": multipolarity from the periphery

Our own commenter from Trinidad and Tobago made the argument better than a script would: multipolarity "looks like the way out of US dominance… when in truth it just means we have multiple masters." Build the episode around the class character of the rival states rather than a moral ranking of empires, and answer the "RCI is a front for imperialism" charge directly.
**Why now:** Second Thought's China video generated the week's largest comment reaction anywhere in the corpus, and the re-cut Multi-Polarity talk was our single best-performing upload — the demand is proven on both sides.

### 5. Solidarity or slogans: what defending Cuba actually requires

Answer the sharpest thing anyone said to us this week — "do you just like shouting 'down with imperialism' and doing nothing else?" — on camera. The mechanics of the blockade, what material aid exists and how it moves, what the RCI concretely does, and why the restoration measures now on the table make this urgent rather than commemorative.
**Why now:** the Cuba reel was our second-biggest post of the week and it converted attention into a demand for action that we have so far left sitting in the comments.

### 6. Conspiracy is a theory of history — just a bad one

The vacuum left by a discredited press is being filled by conspiracy, and increasingly by antisemitic conspiracy, in our own comment sections as well as our competitors'. Rather than moderating in silence, make the episode: why "who really runs things" is the spontaneous form class consciousness takes when it has no materialist explanation available, and why the substitution of a hidden cabal for a social relation disarms the people who adopt it.
**Why now:** one account carried this material under five of our uploads this week, and the same pattern ran through Novara's and Channel 5's comment sections — this is the second consecutive week it has appeared in this report.
