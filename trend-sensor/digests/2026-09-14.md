### AI extinction as hype cycle

A researcher's resignation from Anthropic, carrying a "10% chance AI kills everyone in 10 years" claim, reached Newsnight through Geoffrey Hinton. Novara ran it across four YouTube videos and three Instagram posts; the Hinton reel alone drew 18,071 likes and 1,269 comments. The structural point is that an arms race between competing capitals makes "safety" a speed variable, and the extinction frame shifts attention from present harms (military targeting, water, labour discipline) to a speculative future the same firms sell. Audiences made the move themselves: _"AI is already killing people. It's already heavily used in the Iran invasion governing targeting, including the girls' school"_ (266); _"Michael Walker should watch the Cory Doctorow interview conducted by Michael Walker"_ (69).

## Podcast Angles

### 1. Who deindustrialized the East? The AfD as the price of 1990

Our only post this week drew the question: _"What has the left done to prevent this?"_ The Marxist answer starts with the Treuhand's liquidation of East German industry and Die Linke's decades of administering decline in coalition. It ends where Novara's own commenters already are: _"the demolition of wages and public services"_ led to the AfD. **Why now:** the result is fresh, a question sits unanswered under our own post, and competitor coverage framed it as a "shock" that its own audience rejected (100 likes).

### 2. The GDR's ghost: why "communism" in the East means the state that fell

One commenter argued the East votes AfD *because* it lived under "Die Linke’s original". Another called Germany "former communist (illiberal)", and a third called communism history's biggest killer. An episode on what the SED state actually was (a bureaucratic regime, not workers' power) would take on the frame the entire East German result is being read through. **Why now:** three of our six text comments this week hinge on this equation, and nobody in the landscape distinguishes Stalinism from socialism when covering the AfD.

### 3. Capital leaves by jet; workers are met at the harbour

In the same week, a hedge-fund billionaire moved to Greece to a chorus of "good riddance". Hundreds of masked men confronted a dinghy of 120 people at Portsmouth, and a crowd banged on the doors of a Pakistani cricket team. Our commenter raised it from the right: _"the problem would be Global Migration"_. The episode is capital's freedom of movement versus labour's, and why the far right polices only one. **Why now:** the two stories ran side by side on Novara without being connected, and our audience is already asking the structural question from the wrong end.

### 4. Between revolutions: answering "pick up a gun"

_"If you guys are “Revolutionary” then pick up a gun and do some Revolutionary work"_ this week; _"What can we do? We need action!!"_ last week. Meanwhile the far right mobilizes crowds within hours by livestream, and DSA wins primaries through the Democratic Party. What does a revolutionary organization do in a non-revolutionary period, and why is it neither adventurism nor electoralism? **Why now:** our recruitment reel is still out-gaining our analysis two weeks later (+375 vs +207). The audience is responding to the organizational ask, and the demand for an answer is showing up in our comments.
