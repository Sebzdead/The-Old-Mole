## Podcast Angles

### 1. Why Stalin is different: answering our own comment section

Our best-liked criticism this week: _"so the capitalists r lying abt Lenin but Stalin is where you draw the line?"_ (37 likes), plus _"The CIA used to spread Trotskyite literature"_ (81). The answer is materialist, not moral. It covers a workers' state isolated and degenerating, the rise of a bureaucratic caste, Thermidor, and why the ruling class's hostility to Lenin and its use of Stalin as a scarecrow serve the same purpose. Democracy At Work's "state capitalism vs real socialism" episode shows the question is live across the landscape. **Why now:** our highest-converting post ever in this series (17.6%, 144 comments) opened this argument, and the thread is waiting for our answer.

### 2. "Totalitarian": the career of a Cold War word

One commenter noticed that a Nazi general was the source a BBC historian used for the label. Another pointed out that anarchists held a rally for Kropotkin's funeral under the supposedly "first totalitarian state". This week the liberal states that use the word were authorising spycops to sleep with activists, arresting a lawyer for speaking at a county meeting, and stripping filmmakers of citizenship. Who actually polices speech, and whose interests does the category serve? **Why now:** the historians' framing is the premise of our best-performing reel, and the landscape is full of fresh evidence of how liberal states treat dissent.

### 3. Lenin and the Celtic pact: the right of nations to self-determination, at home

Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland are demanding _"the right to decide"_ (111 likes), not independence as such. That is Lenin's position almost word for word, and he wrote it against the British and Russian empires. What should Marxists say about the break-up of an imperial state? Where does it weaken British capital, and where does it only swap Westminster for Holyrood? **Why now:** the Lenin reel just showed our audience wants the Bolshevik tradition applied, and Novara's own commenters complained it drifted onto Reform instead of engaging with the question.

### 4. The ruling ideas: who owns the story?

A billionaire stadium owner decides what a pop star may say. Two crypto donors fund a party "for nothing". A shelf of "historians" decides Lenin was a tyrant. Our commenters are already making the connection (_"'There's no propaganda in western countries'😂"_), and so are Novara's (_"These are not 'Donors', they are investors"_, 200 likes). The episode is Marx's _German Ideology_ applied to 2026: the class that owns the means of production owns the means of mental production. **Why now:** three of the week's biggest landscape stories and our own top reel are the same story told four ways, and nobody in the landscape is connecting them.
